Athena Governance Architecture

An AI Governance Architecture for Accountable Permission Before Consequential AI Output and Execution.

Athena governs the transition from legitimate human or institutional Authority to bounded, case-specific AI Permission before consequential AI output or execution proceeds.

Capability is not Permission. Technical capability, model confidence, system access, prior success, or workflow inclusion do not by themselves create substantive decision authority.

What Athena Addresses

AI systems increasingly generate recommendations, influence institutional decisions, coordinate workflows, use tools, and execute actions with real-world consequences. Athena addresses a distinct governance question: whether an AI system has legitimate, case-specific Permission to produce or execute the next consequential output or action under the evidence, Authority, consequence, safeguards, scope, and uncertainty that exist in the present case.

Current architecture line: Athena Governance Architecture v0.3

Authority → Permission

Substantive Authority originates from legitimate human or institutional governance. AI Permission is bounded, case-specific, and cannot exceed the Authority Boundary.

Path A / Path B / Path C

Path A preserves bounded automation, Path B seeks targeted clarification, and Path C applies the required Runtime Enforcement conditions followed by Authority Return when the AI must not continue as the substantive decision authority.

Runtime Enforcement

Runtime Enforcement applies the output, execution, restriction, hold, expiration, or Authority Return conditions required by the selected Path. It does not select the Path, reinterpret Threshold Judgment, create Permission, or expand Permission.

Every Path produces and extends an append-only Governance Record. The record preserves the consequential governance trajectory so later review can reconstruct why AI output or execution was allowed or constrained, why the unresolved matter was returned to legitimate human or institutional governance, and whether bounded AI participation was later restored through Controlled Re-entry.

Path A

Proceed Within the Governed Boundary. Bounded AI output or execution may continue when the applicable checklist and approved threshold conditions support continuation within current Permission and the Governed Boundary.

Path B

Targeted Information Expansion and Same-Checklist Re-evaluation. Path B supports targeted clarification where important information or evidence is missing or conflicting. It is not Authority Return.

Path C

Runtime Enforcement and Authority Return. Runtime Enforcement applies the Path C hold, restriction, expiration, or prohibited-action conditions. Authority Return then ends the AI's substantive decision authority under the current conditions and returns the unresolved matter to legitimate human or institutional governance.

Path C, Structured Human Deliberation, and Controlled Re-entry

Path C does not ask a human to endorse an AI conclusion after the fact. Runtime Enforcement applies the required Path C conditions; Authority Return ends the AI's substantive decision authority under the current conditions and returns the unresolved matter to legitimate human or institutional governance.

Candidate Path A / B / C
A candidate Path is not restored Permission. Candidate Path A or B requires explicit, bounded Controlled Re-entry approval before AI participation is restored. Candidate Path C leads to further human review or human-only closure without restored AI Permission.

Governance Record, Controlled Learning, and Controlled Feedback and Revision Governance

Governance Record

Every Path produces and extends an append-only Governance Record that preserves the relevant governance trajectory. Depending on the case, it may preserve evaluation, evidence, Path transitions, Authority Return, re-evaluation, implementation, outcomes, corrections, and other materially relevant states without silently overwriting earlier records.

Controlled Learning

Prior cases may support governed comparison through authorized retrieval. Controlled Learning uses only closed material that has been separately reviewed, approved, and designated as learning-eligible. It does not create current Permission, automatic precedent, or automatic model retraining.

Controlled Feedback and Revision Governance

Feedback is not revision. AI may identify a possible governance gap or support analysis, but it cannot amend or activate governance. Under the current internal working specification, a formal improvement proposal must be human-authored or substantively human-modified, defined, and confirmed. Approved changes remain subject to governed Versioning and Change Control before traceable activation. Historical Governance Records are not silently overwritten.
