Pre-Execution Governance for Case-Specific AI Permission

Athena Governance Architecture examines a core assurance gap: an AI system may be technically capable and operating within an approved workflow while still lacking legitimate, case-specific Permission to produce or execute a consequential outcome under present conditions.

Capability is not Permission. Athena focuses on whether AI action remains within legitimate human or institutional Authority before difficult-to-repair effects occur.

About Athena

Athena Governance Architecture is an independent AI governance research project focused on the transition from legitimate human or institutional Authority to case-specific AI Permission.

The architecture is designed to preserve bounded automation while making it possible to restrict, pause, escalate, or return substantive authority before consequential execution. It connects pre-execution evaluation with runtime control, explicit re-entry conditions, reconstructable governance records, and controlled revision of the governance instruments themselves.

Athena does not treat human presence as sufficient evidence of meaningful oversight. Its concern is whether relevant people and institutions retain practical agency to pause, narrow, refuse, revise, or close AI-supported action before responsibility is assigned without real power.

Research Focus

Capability and Permission

Technical capability, system access, and workflow inclusion do not by themselves create case-specific Permission to act.

Assurance Before Execution

Governance must become operational before difficult-to-repair effects occur, not only through post hoc review.

Runtime Enforcement

Governance decisions must change what a system is allowed to output, execute, hold, limit, or defer in the active case.

Governance Record Reconstructability

Reviewers should be able to reconstruct why action was allowed, constrained, escalated, or resumed.

Controlled Re-entry

A later recommendation to resume does not automatically restore AI Permission; limited re-entry requires explicit, bounded approval.

Human-Controlled Revision

AI may support revision proposals, but active governance instruments change only through human review, controlled testing, approval, versioning, and traceable activation.

Integrated Governance Architecture

Athena connects assurance evaluation to runtime consequences while keeping current-case control separate from future governance change. Current Status

Athena is under controlled development as a research architecture. Its current work includes refinement of the common harm-and-event analysis checklist baseline, controlled case testing, evaluation of escalation quality and review burden, and further specification of reconstructable governance records and revision controls.

Athena is not presented as an independently validated deployment, certification, compliance guarantee, legal opinion, or universal substantive decision framework.
